# Metadata Template
Metadata Sovereignty Alliance – Record Documentation Template

This template provides a standard structure for recording authorship, provenance, and governance information when creating or analysing records.

The aim is to ensure records remain:

• traceable  
• interpretable  
• ethically governed  
• accessible to both humans and AI systems.

Students should use this template when documenting research artefacts, example records, or case studies. The examples below are illustrative, not evidence of consent or permission. Follow [data classification and public sharing rules](DATA_CLASSIFICATION.md); do not place real sensitive records or identifying information in this public repository.

This human-readable guide does not replace the [version 0.1 research-record contract](docs/MACHINE_OPERABLE_RESEARCH_SYSTEM.md). When an artefact enters a governed workflow, use that contract and its schema for the required machine-readable fields and values; do not assume the examples below map directly to its controlled values.

# Record Metadata

## Record Title
Name or short description of the record.

Example  
Community Advocacy Meeting Notes – Housing Support Case

---

## Author

Name of the person who created the original record.

Example  
Jane Smith

---

## Author Role

Describe the author’s role.

Examples  
Self-advocate  
Researcher  
Community worker  
Legal advocate

---

## Creation Date

Date the record was first created.

Format

YYYY-MM-DD

Example  
2026-03-15

---

## Document Purpose

Explain why the record was created.

Examples

• documenting an advocacy case  
• recording meeting outcomes  
• summarising research findings  
• recording lived-experience testimony

---

## Consent Status

Indicate whether consent exists for use of the record.

Possible values

Consent granted  
Consent limited  
Consent withdrawn  
Consent unknown

Record the permitted purposes and limits, and reference the relevant consent evidence where appropriate without exposing protected information. A status label alone does not establish valid consent or authorise every use. Where consent or its scope is unknown, do not infer permission.

---

## Version Number

Track record versions.

Examples

v1.0 – original record  
v1.1 – minor edits  
v2.0 – major revision

---

## Change History

Record edits made to the document.

| Version | Date | Author | Change Description |
|--------|------|------|--------------------|
| v1.0 | 2026-03-15 | Jane Smith | Original record created |
| v1.1 | 2026-03-18 | Jane Smith | Minor corrections |

---

## Source References

List supporting sources or related documents.

Examples

• meeting recordings  
• research papers  
• policy documents  
• case documentation

---

## Accessibility Formats

Indicate whether accessible formats are available.

Examples

Easy-Read version available  
AAC summary available  
Plain language summary available  
Captioned media available

---

## Data Sensitivity

Describe the sensitivity level of the record.

Examples

Public  
Internal use  
Confidential  
Highly sensitive

---

## AI Processing Permissions

Indicate whether the record can be processed by AI systems.

Possible values

Allowed for summarisation  
Allowed for accessibility formatting  
Not permitted for AI processing

Record permissions separately for each proposed use and any applicable limits or source references. Consent for one purpose does not automatically permit another, including model training or commercial use. Missing permission is not permission. The structured contract requires explicit AI permission fields; see the [machine-operable guidance](docs/MACHINE_OPERABLE_RESEARCH_SYSTEM.md) and [AI boundaries](AI_BOUNDARIES_PUBLIC.md). A declaration in this template does not itself grant authority.

# Why Metadata Matters

Strong metadata ensures records can remain:

• understandable years later  
• auditable and accountable  
• ethically governed  
• interoperable with other systems

This template supports the **metadata-first approach** being explored in this project.
