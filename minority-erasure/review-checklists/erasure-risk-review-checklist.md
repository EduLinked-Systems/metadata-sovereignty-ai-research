# Erasure Risk Review Checklist

Use this checklist before publishing, summarising, classifying, translating, training on, or reusing minority-authored or lived-experience material.

## Authorship

1. Is the author identified or protected in metadata?
2. Is the author's role clear?
3. Has institutional authorship replaced lived-experience authorship?
4. Is attribution still visible after summarisation or format conversion?

## Consent

1. Is consent scope recorded?
2. Is the intended use inside that scope?
3. Are audience, purpose, format, and time limits clear?
4. Is withdrawal supported?
5. If consent is missing or unclear, has the system stopped publication?

## Context

1. Has cultural, disability, language, access, family, service, geographic, or power context been preserved where relevant?
2. Has uncertainty been preserved?
3. Has the material been made more comfortable for an institution by removing the author's meaning?

## Accessibility

1. Are required access formats recorded?
2. Are those formats included in the publication plan?
3. Has accessibility been reviewed by a human where required?
4. Has the system avoided claiming conformance from automated checks alone?

## AI Use

1. Is AI use disclosed?
2. Is the AI action allowed by the record?
3. Has AI inferred consent, authority, or community representation?
4. Has a human reviewed meaning, consent, accessibility, and publication authority?

## Decision

| Condition | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Authorship missing | Stop or escalate. |
| Consent missing or unclear | Treat as no permission. |
| Accessibility formats missing | Mark accessibility erasure risk. |
| AI changed meaning | Stop and require human review. |
| Human review incomplete | Do not publish as authoritative evidence. |
