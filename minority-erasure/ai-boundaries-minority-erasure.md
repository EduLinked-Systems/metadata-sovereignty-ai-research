# AI Boundaries for Minority Erasure

This document defines boundaries for AI systems that process minority-authored, lived-experience, accessibility, advocacy, or community-governance material.

## AI May Assist With

1. Identifying missing metadata fields.
2. Flagging possible erasure risks for human review.
3. Generating plain language drafts from approved source material.
4. Checking whether required access formats are listed.
5. Comparing a draft summary against a source for meaning loss.
6. Producing synthetic examples for testing governance workflows.

## AI Must Not Do Alone

1. Decide whether a lived-experience record is safe to publish.
2. Infer consent when consent metadata is absent.
3. Replace an author's meaning with institutional language.
4. Flatten identity, disability, culture, language, class, or context to simplify a dataset.
5. Convert a private or restricted record into a public training example.
6. Claim community representation without authority from that community.
7. Treat accessibility supports as optional formatting preferences.

## Required Fail-Closed Conditions

AI-assisted workflows should stop or mark the output as not publishable when:

1. Authorship is missing or contradictory.
2. Consent scope is missing, expired, withdrawn, or unclear.
3. Human review is required but incomplete.
4. Accessibility requirements are missing from the publication plan.
5. The summary changes meaning, authority, uncertainty, or attribution.
6. The material could identify a person or group without valid publication authority.

## Output Labelling

AI-assisted outputs should clearly state:

1. Whether AI was used.
2. What AI did.
3. What source material was used.
4. Whether a human reviewed it.
5. Whether publication authority exists.
6. Whether unresolved erasure risks remain.
