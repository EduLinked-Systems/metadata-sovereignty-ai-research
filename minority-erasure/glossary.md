# Minority Erasure Glossary

## Minority Erasure

Minority erasure occurs when a person or group from a marginalised, under-represented, or structurally disadvantaged community is removed, flattened, misclassified, de-authored, or made less visible in a record, system, summary, dataset, policy process, or public narrative.

Erasure can happen even when no single person intends harm.

## Authorship Erasure

Authorship erasure occurs when the originator of an idea, story, analysis, creative work, or lived-experience record is not named, is misnamed, is replaced by an institution, or is treated as a source of raw material rather than as an author.

## Context Erasure

Context erasure occurs when important social, cultural, historical, disability, access, language, geographic, or power context is removed in a way that changes meaning.

## Consent Erasure

Consent erasure occurs when material is reused outside the scope, purpose, audience, format, time period, or conditions that the author or affected community agreed to.

## Accessibility Erasure

Accessibility erasure occurs when access needs, communication supports, plain language, Easy Read, AAC, captions, Auslan, screen-reader structure, or other required formats are omitted or treated as optional afterthoughts.

## Metadata Erasure

Metadata erasure occurs when authorship, provenance, permissions, access needs, version history, review status, or withdrawal conditions are not recorded in a durable and machine-readable way.

## Translation Erasure

Translation erasure occurs when a person or group is represented only through another speaker, institution, summariser, or AI system, and the translation changes authority, tone, meaning, uncertainty, or lived-experience significance.

## Institutional Reframing

Institutional reframing occurs when lived experience is rewritten to protect, promote, simplify, or legitimise an organisation instead of preserving the author's meaning and authority.

## Synthetic Example

A synthetic example is an invented record used for testing, training, demonstration, or documentation. It must not be based on an identifiable person without consent.

## Human Review

Human review means accountable review by a person with appropriate authority, context, and responsibility. Automated validation can support review, but cannot replace it.
