# Easy Read Resources

Easy Read is not a synonym for plain language, short sentences, pictures, or a fixed document template. In the current EduLinked architecture it is treated as a modular, pedagogically informed accessible communication and learning capability that adapts meaning, representation, sequencing and support to the person, purpose and context. A static Easy Read document is one possible projection of that capability.

For EduLinked operational use, inspect the existing source-owned authority chain first:

- `EduLinked-Pty-Ltd/core-machine-objects/objects/education/easy-read-adaptive-instructional-composition-v0.1.json` — current Easy Read semantic/composition authority.
- `EduLinked-Systems/edulinked-accessibility-system/adapters/easy-read/entrypoints.yaml` — current implementation/navigation projection.
- `EduLinked-Pty-Ltd/core-machine-objects/objects/accessibility-capability-layer/contracts/accessible-document-lifecycle-v1.json` — source-preserving accessible-document lifecycle.
- `EduLinked-Pty-Ltd/accessible-RTO/templates/inclusive-learning-production-standard.md` — AccessFlow v1.2.0 inclusive production requirements.

`EduLinked-Systems/easy-read` is a successor boundary with an open source-owned authority-proof gate. Do not treat it as proven production authority until that gate is resolved.

## Research characteristics to examine

Depending on the person, purpose and context, an Easy Read composition may involve:

- meaning preservation and explicit explanation
- vocabulary familiarity
- information density and chunk size
- sequencing and scaffolding
- prior-knowledge demand
- cognitive-load and memory support
- visual or symbol-supported representation where appropriate
- AAC interoperability where appropriate
- alternate representation or modality
- comprehension checks and supported conversation
- human mediation or specialist review where required

A specific person or cohort may not need or prefer every modality. `ACCESSIBILITY_OPTION_AVAILABLE != PARTICIPANT_PREFERENCE`.

## Research Topics for Students

Students researching Easy Read should examine:

- comprehension and semantic preservation
- document and interaction structure
- cognitive accessibility
- representation and visual communication
- AAC/symbol interoperability without equating symbols with AAC
- language and localisation
- how Easy Read supports advocacy, rights communication and learning
- how actual user validation differs from automated readability or structural checking

Preserve these claim ceilings:

- `PLAIN_LANGUAGE != EASY_READ`
- `PICTURES_PLUS_SHORT_SENTENCES != EASY_READ`
- `MACHINE_GENERATED != HUMAN_VALIDATED_EASY_READ`
- `AUTOMATED_CHECK != ACCESSIBILITY_CONFORMANCE`
- `COMPLIANCE_OUTPUT != COMPREHENSION_EVIDENCE`
